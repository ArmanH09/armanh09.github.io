Our Policy For Clean Energy

The government should be responsible for providing panels to everyone to ensure clean and available energy. As global temperatures continue to rise, electricity is becoming a necessity for survival. Without sources of electricity people are forced to endure extreme heat without air conditioning. This is why electricity should be provided to everyone. Additionally, since existing rights like freedom of speech on the internet require electricity, power must be provided to protect these rights. The best way to ensure access to energy is through panels because they are safe, clean and reliable.

Natural rights are rights that are given to everyone at birth, these are inalienable rights. The United States has a responsibility to protect these rights, which include life, liberty and property. This matters because as humanity evolves, our needs grow. For example, to sustain life electricity is needed to fuel homes, businesses and schools. If electricity is not available this right is restricted. That shows that energy can be considered a right even though it was not explicitly mentioned in the Constitution. Energy is a new need but it's just as important as the rights already recognized. The Declaration of Independence emphasizes a contract, where the government gets its authority from the people. It also supports sovereignty meaning people have a say in how they are governed. As energy becomes essential for survival people can advocate for a law that recognizes energy access as a right.

Because this is true the federal government can uphold this law through the Necessary & Proper Clause. This clause allows the government to take actions that are not specifically listed in the Constitution but are necessary to carry out its implied powers. The power to provide energy can be considered an implied power because it helps citizens protect their rights. This works along with the Commerce Clause. If people dont have electricity, businesses cannot run. This affects both intrastate commerce. So without energy economic activity slows down. The Constitution supports the idea that energy is needed by every citizen.

Energy is a topic that has divided parties. According to the Pew Research Center in 2022 about 54% of Republicans supported energy production. In the last four years that number has dropped to nearly 44%. On the other hand, Democrats have stayed supportive with about 85% supporting renewable energy, only a 2% decrease since 2022. Republicans are more likely to say that solar energy is worse for the environment, more expensive and less reliable than Democrats. This shows how each party views the issue differently. The California Democratic Party supports a system that's renewable and better for the environment. This policy would help the party by increasing the number of solar panels across the country and boosting renewable energy. Also according to the Pew Research Center 12% of Republicans see climate change as a top priority. Republicans value climate change in most areas unless it is connected to religious values as shown by the Yale Climate Change Communication. This shows that the Republican platform is not focused on climate change and favors fuels.

If this amendment is added it will take effort from every part of the country. This is the idea of federalism, a system where power is shared between the government and state governments. In terms of energy the Regulatory Assistance Project says energy regulation uses federalism. States oversee resource planning, infrastructure investment and cost allocation. Regional grid operators plan for system reliability across states. This shared responsibility shows how different levels of government can work together.

For our policy we will argue for an amendment that treats access to energy as a right. We want to require all state and local governments to work to supply solar panels to every community. We want to make sure no home is left without electricity. We will use federalism to do this. The federal government will have the power to deal with energy companies. These companies will be required to make an amount of energy available to every building. The federal government will also provide funding to ensure all homes get panels. Using these funds state governments will distribute energy to grids based on population and need. Local governments will get the money. Use it to install solar panels in homes. This shows how cooperation between all levels of government is needed to ensure energy access. We will partner with human rights groups like the American Civil Liberties Union to build support. We will reach out to district officials. Bring our plan to Congress. To make sure the policy works we will require states to report each year on how many communities have panels in all buildings. We will keep monitoring until every community has a reliable energy network.

One possible constitutional concern is the Fifth Amendment, which protects property rights. A homeowner might argue that being forced to install panels interferes with their property. This becomes an issue if the installation greatly affects their property. A solution is to make the installation voluntary. Getting permission from homeowners or landlords will prevent conflict. Make the move toward sustainability easier.

It can be argued that requiring panels for all communities will be too expensive. It is clear that solar energy is cheaper than fossil fuels in the long run. Over time this will save communities money on their utility bills. That means more money will be available for uses, like business and personal spending. So even if there is a cost, the savings later will benefit everyone.
